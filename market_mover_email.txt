#1 — Impact: 9.5/10
  US-Iran Peace Talks Resume as Ceasefire Expiry Looms April 21
  Source: CNBC / Bloomberg
  A second round of U.S.-Iran negotiations is being arranged in Islamabad with Trump saying talks could happen 'in the next two days,' just days before the April 21 ceasefire expiry. The Strait of Hormuz remains at ~10% of normal throughput (~2.1M bbl/day), keeping WTI crude near $91/barrel. A deal would collapse oil prices and lift airlines, consumer stocks, and rate-cut prospects; a breakdown would spike oil toward $100+, hitting energy-importing sectors hard and locking the Fed into a hawkish hold.
  Read: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/15/oil-price-us-iran-talks-hormuz-trump.html

#2 — Impact: 8.0/10
  Big Banks Post Blowout Q1 Earnings; JPMorgan Trading Revenue Hits All-Time Record
  Source: Yahoo Finance / CNBC
  JPMorgan beat estimates with EPS of $5.94 (vs $5.45 est.) and record trading revenue of $11.6B (+20% YoY), though it trimmed full-year net interest income guidance to $103B from $104.5B. BlackRock reported +27% revenue growth with record ETF inflows, while Wells Fargo and Citi also beat. Today Bank of America and Morgan Stanley report. Broad beats signal the economy is weathering the Iran war shock, boosting financials and lifting sentiment—but JPMorgan's NII cut is a warning on rate-cut delays.
  Read: https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/jpmorgan-and-other-big-banks-see-profits-rise-as-dimon-warns-of-increasingly-complex-set-of-risks-105717454.html

#3 — Impact: 7.5/10
  March PPI Comes In Far Below Forecast, Fueling Hope for Fed Rate Cuts
  Source: CNBC / BLS
  The March Producer Price Index rose just 0.5% MoM (vs 1.1% consensus) and core PPI a mere 0.1% (vs 0.5% expected), a massive inflation undershoot despite war-driven energy prices. Gasoline surged 15.7% and diesel 42%, yet services prices were flat, suggesting energy is the only inflation culprit. If the Iran ceasefire holds and energy normalizes, the Fed—currently on hold at 3.5–3.75%—would have clear cover to cut, driving a potential rally in rate-sensitive sectors: REITs, utilities, housing, and bonds.
